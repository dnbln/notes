## Site Description

To create a building that fits within the area it is important to have a good understanding of the current state of the area. Our site is located, only a six-minute walk to Rotterdam Central Station. The highest building (green roof on the left) is twenty-three meters tall. The other buildings are between thirteen and eighteen meters tall. Most buildings have a 14 metre base (3 to 7 meters) between the building and the parking lot. Its location is close to various means of public transport. It takes 4 minutes to walk to the tram and bus stop Weena. East of the site is an adjacent biking lane and cars can reach the location on the west side via Delftseplein.

There are a variety of services that you can find in the area we will list a few of them that are relevant for the later analysis of our building:

  

### Schieblock

The Schieblock is a vibrant multi-purpose building with two main components: studios and semi-public spaces. The studios are designed for focused work and provide space for numerous creative entrepreneurs and artists. The semi-public spaces serve as areas for meet-ups, interaction, and performance. These include restaurant TEDS, the rooftop farm DakAkker, and the ground floor with BAR, Urban Guides, OMI, and workspace MESS. The Luchtsingel bridge cuts through the Schieblock and leads to the Biergarten. External parties can also use the semi-public spaces, encouraging maximum interaction between the city and the Schieblock. The Schieblock is an initiative of the Rotterdam municipality, the building's owner, and the architecture firm ZUS. Currently, VPS Nederland manages the Schieblock and is responsible for the management, security, maintenance, and rental of the various studios.

  

### Bierkade

Biergarten Rotterdam is a lively, seasonal 'beer garden' with burgers, snacks and a large selection of craft beers on tap. You eat freshly grilled food and beer. You listen to DJs and live music and on Friday nights to the biergarten soundsystem of Rotterdam. 

  

### Annabel

Annabel is the largest pop venue in Rotterdam. At Annabel you can see and hear international, leading acts, from hip-hop to pop to electronic. It is a lively place to have a beer on the terrace or just to dance during one of the many parties or festivals. Or listen to music at one of the concerts.

  

There are also many restaurants, sports facilities, and cultural facilities surrounding the area. This brings us to one of the main questions in the process of initialization: how can our building become a valuable addition to the area?

  

## Vision

The aim is to procedurally design a community-oriented and sustainable building that enhances the current Schieblock area. The proposed building, UNITY, will provide 400 student units, 200 elderly units and 200 starter units. Through the implementation of different layers of privacy, we create spaces that facilitate different levels of community engagement. A few examples of these spaces are agonistic spaces of encounter, shared working spaces, and a public library. 

  

In our design process, we consider the following key pillars to base our decisions on for the building design.

  

- Community engagement
    
- Sustainability
    
- Noise pollution
    
- Wind pollution
    
- Maximizing sunlight
    

  

## User Scenarios

Our building is home to six different types of residents.

  

### Student in single unit

“As a student, I enjoy having my own space. I don’t even have to leave the building to access community spaces such as an open library, shared workspaces and a cafeteria. I like that the community center organizes various activities for all ages. When I leave the building I can go through the bikeshed. Even though I can’t have pets myself, there is always a cat around.”

  

### Student in multi-unit

“Sharing a space already creates a sense of community. There is always someone in the hallway to talk to. I also like that, I can find my privacy in my own room. I can go to the community kitchen for a meal. Similarly, we can enjoy the shared workspaces and the organized activities.”

  

### Independent Elderly

“It is a very lively environment. Every Tuesday I can go to the café and read a book from the open library. The care center and GP in the building are convenient for any of my medical concerns. I babysit my neighbours’ kid every Friday. I love going to the movies. My dog is in good care at the animal care center. My apartment is directly connected to the private parking for cars and bikes in the building."

  

### Assisted Elderly

“The building is well adjusted for the assistance I need. With the care center and GP in the building, I am in great care. I like the ground floor units with my own entrance through my little garden. I am glad that there are so many agonistic spaces of encounter that keep me engaged and connected. Every day, there is a nice meal in the community kitchen. Sometimes the students join in as well.”

  

### Starters - Family

“We can take the elevator down to the common space where there are fun activities for our children. The plaza/outdoor communal space is designed in such a way that it is a playground for children. Our young child needs regular consultation. The access to the GP is perfect. It is very convenient to have students living in the same building because they can babysit our child.”

  

### Starters - Couples

“My partner and I both work a lot. We really like the shared working spaces. They allow us to not have to go to the office every day but still find a quiet place. Even better is the fact that we have a care center in the building to stay active. I am happy that there is a rooftop garden for our cat to go to. And the animal care center makes it easy to get the best care for our animals. We also like the private cinema and spa for our dates. The building has a lot to offer.”

## Design program

### Considerations

To cater towards all the needs of these residents we designed a design program that adds on to the facilities that are already in the area. The analysis is based on a spatial analysis in which we looked at what facilities were already within a 5-minute distance by foot. As previously mentioned, we want to support community engagement but provide enough privacy for residents. There will be different layers of privacy. To create a sustainable building we included a water tank that provides water that can be reused for toilets, washing machines, and dishes but also more filtered water for bath and shower use. In addition, the water tank has a storage capacity for a flood event that occurs once in 10 years and sustains for 9 days to alleviate water pressure for the surrounding area. On top of that, the building needs to have as much green space as possible. Green spaces help with heat regulation and improve mental health. This is integrated into our building as green roofs and keeping the trees surrounding the area as much as possible. The building also takes four considerations into account. First, we want to take into account noise pollution that comes from the Biergarten, Annabel, and the train tracks when placing the programs. Second, we want to maximize the amount of sunlight every residential unit receives. By Dutch regulations, all residential units should have at least 2 hours of sunlight a day. Third, we need to minimize the amount of sun that is blocked by the building. The surrounding buildings should be minimally affected by our building. Fourth, we want to minimize the level of wind pollution created by the building. 

  
  

### The program

Based on our considerations we defined a total of 16 programs on top of the required student units, elderly units, and starter units. The first level of privacy starts in the residential area. Starters and independent elderly have different privacy needs than students and assisted elderly. For students, we have created single units and multi-units. Within the multi-units, there is the possibility to live with four or eight different students. For the elderly units, we have differences between assisted elderly units and independent elderly units. Then for starters, we created two categories. There are units for couples and units for small, new families of three people. The second layer of privacy is where residents meet each other. To enhance community engagement among residents, we implemented a community kitchen where assisted elderly can get meals daily but it is also open for students to use. Agonistic spaces of encounter are created on every floor. These are in-between connective spaces where people can meet each other. A community contact center is created for residents to organize activities. The next layer of privacy is where people from outside are welcome too. A cafeteria, open library, market and informal space, and cinema are open to use for a wider public. This way the building not only contributes to its residents but also facilitates the residents of the neighboring neighborhoods in the area. Furthermore, we added some facilities that improve living enjoyability and accessibility such as the Care Center, GP, private and public parking for bikes and cars, a package delivery point, and an animal care center.
